5/17/26, 17:17 - Saruabh: And here is what I think of it:

1. What it is: An inner product is just a generalized dot product —> a way to measure two things between vectors: how long they are and how aligned they are. ⟨u, v⟩ = |u||v|cos θ. That's it.

Why we need it: Without it vectors are just lists of numbers. The inner product is what gives us geometry --> length (‖v‖² = ⟨v,v⟩), angle, and orthogonality. In next lecture we will be learning projections which are built on these concepts.. 

2. Why "symmetric + positive definite" A? 
The standard dot product assumes all axes are equal and perpendicular. But if your features live on different scales (say income in lakhs vs age in years), the plain dot product is misleading. Replacing I with a matrix A in ⟨u, v⟩= uᵀAv, lets you define a custom geometry suited to your data. The two conditions just ensure the geometry stays sensible: symmetric → ⟨u,v⟩ = ⟨v,u⟩, and positive definite → lengths stay positive (⟨v,v⟩ > 0 for v ≠ 0). Mahalanobis distance is exactly this idea.

In the next lecture I'll tie this back to projections that's where the "why" really clicks.
